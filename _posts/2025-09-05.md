🎓 Achievement Unlocked: Master of Statistics 🎓

I have been working on this personal and professional project since Fall 2020.  I am thrilled to share that I have graduated.  The journey was never mine alone. I had an incredible supporting cast that helped make this possible, and I want to pause and give thanks.

To God.  I do not fully know His plans yet—but I do know this: doors opened that only He could open, and others closed that weren’t mine to walk through. Today I give thanks to God, as I’m called to do:

Colossians 3:17 — “And whatever you do, whether in word or deed, do it all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through Him.”

To my wife.  None of this would have happened without Elizabeth’s support on the home front. Those were some tough years, but everyone made it through to the other side of this venture. Your patience and perseverance will bear fruit.

To my mentors:
Gina Reed — My most influential professor at the University of North Georgia, and the one who lit the fire for statistics in me.
Benkam Bobga — My advisor at UNG, a beloved professor whose guidance was invaluable.
Andrew Burford — My former supervisor at Cardlytics, whose counsel I sought before even applying to graduate school.

Each of you not only guided me but also wrote letters of recommendation when I applied to NC State. That trust meant everything. Whenever the work got difficult, I thought of you—your reputation staked to my success. I couldn’t let you down. Your faith in me, together with Ephesians 4:1-2’s call to “walk worthy of the calling you have received,” kept me pushing forward. I hope I’ve made you proud.

To Suzanne Bullard. Colloquially, my “Math Buddy.” Suzanne had a huge impact on me during my undergraduate years. Our friendly competitiveness pushed me academically—so much so that it carried over into my time at NC State, shaping how I tested, took notes, and engaged with the material. One of the best pieces of advice I can give to anyone pursuing success is to surround yourself with intelligent, mature peers.

Commencement ceremonies are not until December.  I will be in Raleigh, North Carolina for it, but between now and then I plan to catch up with some people over the next few weeks–and get around to a few things I have been putting off these last few years in lieu of studies.  Formal education ends but the work is just beginning.  Wherever do is opened to me next, my commitment is to “walk worthy” of the calling.
