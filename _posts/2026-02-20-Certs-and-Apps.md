I have been fairly quiet this past year. Sometimes you get so focused on the work directly in front of you that you forget to look up and notice how much the world around you is moving. That is what life has felt like since the spring of 2025 — head down, steady progress, and constant change happening all at once.

Last spring, I took a Python programming course focused on machine learning at NC State. It was taught by Prof. Dr. Justin Post, and I highly recommend it to anyone in the statistics program. The course was genuinely fun — the kind where you keep working long after you planned to stop because you want to understand just one more concept. It was also the first class where I truly felt the pressure to solve problems on my own rather than lean on AI tools. Ironically, AI remains incredibly helpful when tracking down stubborn traceback errors, but the learning came from wrestling with the ideas myself.

Then came my final semester and the capstone project — the work that eventually became my portfolio paper, *“Bridging the Gap.”* I was paired with two outstanding teammates, and together we tackled a real-world survey analytics problem. The project was a success, and for the first time I felt the satisfaction of applying statistical training to a practical, messy, real-world dataset. It was challenging, collaborative, and deeply rewarding.

And then — graduation.

Two months later, I was laid off from a job I had held for eight and a half years.

That moment was disruptive in ways both practical and emotional. Financially, it introduced some pressure, but it also forced a pause after nearly six straight years of nonstop momentum. For the first time in a long while, I slowed down. I spent more time with my wife and kids, worked on projects around the house that had long been postponed, and allowed myself a brief reset.

About six weeks in, though, the familiar itch returned — the desire to build, learn, and move forward again. I wrapped up a new project for GitHub and enrolled in the Google Advanced Data Analytics certificate on Coursera, which I recently completed. It felt good to reestablish rhythm and direction.

So this past year has been a mix of milestones and uncertainty: graduating from NC State, navigating a layoff, investing in family time, sharpening technical skills, and applying for roles in a challenging job market — all while trying to maintain perspective and optimism.

Looking ahead, I am working on several projects focused on machine learning applications in financial services, including fraud detection, banking customer churn, and mortgage default prediction. These are problems that sit at the intersection of statistics, decision-making, and real-world impact — exactly where I want to be.

I know I can do these things.

Now it is about showing it.
