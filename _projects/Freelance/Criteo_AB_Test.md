### Project Overview

This project analyzes a large-scale **A/B testing dataset from Criteo**, focusing on measuring the causal impact of advertising exposure on user conversion behavior. The primary objective was to determine whether targeted advertising meaningfully increases conversion rates, while accounting for selection bias and treatment effects.

The analysis follows a structured experimental and statistical workflow, beginning with data exploration and validation of treatment/control group balance, followed by estimation of treatment effects using classical statistical methods and uplift modeling techniques.

Special emphasis was placed on distinguishing **incremental lift** from raw conversion differences, highlighting the importance of causal inference in marketing analytics. The project demonstrates how naive comparisons can misrepresent impact, and how proper experimental design and statistical reasoning lead to more accurate conclusions.

Visualizations and summary statistics were used throughout to communicate findings clearly, making the results accessible to both technical and non-technical stakeholders.

### Key Outcomes

- Evaluated the effectiveness of advertising using controlled experimental data
- Quantified **incremental lift** between treatment and control groups
- Demonstrated the difference between correlation and causation in marketing data
- Validated experimental group balance and identified potential biases
- Applied statistical reasoning to interpret real-world A/B testing results
- Highlighted limitations of naive conversion rate comparisons
